Inflation & Interest Rates

A 66-Year Journey Through Economic Policy (1960–2026)

📈 Normal Relationship

The Fed typically raises interest rates ABOVE inflation to cool the economy. Rates should lead, not follow.

⚠️ The 1970s Mistake

When rates stay BELOW inflation (negative real rates), inflation accelerates. Volcker's aggressive response was the painful cure.

✅ The New Test (2026)

An energy supply shock (Iran conflict) re-ignited inflation while the Fed holds steady — testing whether credibility alone can anchor expectations.

Notable Outliers & Anomalies

⚠️

1981: Peak Volcker (16.4% Fed Rate)

Highest Fed funds rate in history — held ~6 points ABOVE inflation to break inflationary psychology. Unemployment hit 10.8%, but inflation fell from 13.5% to 3.2% by 1983.
⚠️

2021–2022: The Great Lag (8% inflation, 0.1% rates)

The Fed called inflation "transitory" and held near zero as inflation hit 8%. A 7.9-point gap — largest since 1975 — echoing 1970s policy errors.
ℹ️

2008–2015: Zero Lower Bound Era

For 7 years the Fed couldn't cut below zero, relying on QE instead. Positive inflation with near-zero rates showed extreme policy can break normal relationships.
⚠️

2026: The Supply-Shock Dilemma (4.2% peak inflation, Fed on hold)

After three 2025 cuts brought rates to 3.50–3.75%, the Iran conflict's energy shock pushed inflation to 4.2% (May 2026) — highest in 3 years. The Fed has held all year under new Chair Kevin Warsh, with three FOMC members dissenting in favor of a HIKE in July 2026. The dilemma: raising rates can't fix a supply shock, but doing nothing risks un-anchoring expectations.

🎓 Key Educational Takeaways

1.The Taylor Principle: To control inflation, real rates (nominal minus inflation) must be positive. Rates must lead, not follow.

2.Credibility Matters: Volcker's pain in the 1980s bought decades of trust. In 2026 that credibility is being tested by a supply shock the Fed can't directly fix.

3.Policy Lags: Rate changes take 12–18 months to fully hit the economy. The Fed must anticipate, not just react.

4.Demand vs. Supply Shocks: Rate hikes cool demand-driven inflation (2022) well, but are blunt tools against supply shocks (1973 oil, 2026 energy). Different causes need different medicine.

5.Never a Straight Line: 2025's easing cycle (3 cuts) reversed course within months when geopolitics intervened — by mid-2026 markets priced meaningful odds of a hike.

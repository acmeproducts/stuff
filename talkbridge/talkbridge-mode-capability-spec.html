TalkBridge — Mode & Capability Spec

Final ruling · July 2026 · Supersedes "Two Design Insights" · For team review
The ruling

A call is a call. In-call, each person controls their own experience.

There is one room and one surface: the transcript. But the three modes carry different intent. Chat is text-first — the mic only transcribes, never transmits. Tapping phone or video is a deliberate decision to transmit your voice — it behaves like a standard call: the other party is notified, it rings, they answer or decline, and an unanswered call is recorded as a missed call in the transcript.

Sender — one switch

Mic ON: everything flows. Your speech is transcribed and translated into bubbles, always. In a call, your raw voice is also transmitted.

Mic OFF (mute): nothing leaves you. No transcription, no bubbles, no raw voice. Mute means mute — the universal expectation holds.

The mic icon means exactly one thing in every mode. No partial mute, no "still transcribing" state, no second pipeline.

Receiver — two toggles, each person's own

Ear — hear the other party's raw voice. Default ON in a call (a call sounds like a call).

TTS — hear their translation read aloud in your language. Default OFF.

Translation bubbles are always visible regardless of either toggle. During a call each person tunes their own experience: voice only, translation aloud only, both, or read silently. The sender never controls what the receiver hears — only whether anything leaves them at all.

Call lifecycle

Ring, answer, decline, missed

Placing: tapping phone (or video) sends a ring. The caller sees a ringing state on the transcript; the mode icon goes active.

Receiving: the other party gets a notification and an incoming-call overlay with Answer and Decline. Answer connects the call — voice flows both ways by default. Decline dismisses it.

Missed: an unanswered or declined call drops a "Missed call" pill into the transcript, timestamped, visible to both parties.

Ending: hang up de-escalates to chat. The room, the transcript, and everything said during the call stay exactly where they are. Exit (chat mode only) leaves the room to the splash screen.

Transcript pills record the lifecycle: call started, call ended with duration, missed call, [Name] muted / unmuted, camera on / off.

Layout

Top ribbon and control strip

Top ribbon (always visible, never changes)

Left: hamburger menu · green presence dot · room name. Center: three mode icons — chat · phone · video; the active one is teal, inactive are grey; tapping phone or video places a call. Right: single horizontal ellipsis (···) opening the room drawer.

Control strip (below top ribbon, always visible)

Same background and height as the top ribbon. Icons sized identically to the mode icons above. When 3 controls are shown they align directly under the 3 mode icons; when 2 controls are shown they sit at the midpoints between the mode icons. Non-red controls: no background fill, inactive-grey icon color. Red control: red filled circle, white icon.

Chat: mic · exit (red). Exit leaves the room to the splash screen.

Phone: timer with green pulse dot on the left · mic · hang up (red). Hang up de-escalates to chat — it does not leave the room.

Video: timer on the left · mic · camera · hang up (red).

Composer

Typing is always available. Keyboard voice-typing is available only when the control-strip mic is off — the strip mic is the master; when it is on, it owns the audio.

Room drawer (···)

The environment toggles live here: Ear (default on in-call; red slash when off) and TTS (default off; red slash when off). Each person's settings affect only what they themselves hear.

What changed from "Two Design Insights"

Decision record

Insight A rejected. Mute no longer keeps transcription alive. Sender mute is total: no transcription, no transmission. Removes the dual-pipeline complexity and preserves the universal meaning of mute.

Insight B rejected as a mode model, kept as a surface model. One transcript surface with call layers on top stands. But a phone call is not "chat plus an unused channel" — it is a deliberate decision to transmit voice, with standard call semantics: notification, ring, answer/decline, missed call. Ear defaults ON in a call.

Receiver toggles are binary. Ear and TTS are simple on/off toggles (earlier incremental multi-step behavior is dropped). Each person's toggles affect only their own experience.
